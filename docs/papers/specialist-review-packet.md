# Coalgebra/incidence specialist review packet

Status: ready to send; external review not yet obtained.

## Requested judgment

Please assess whether the proposed novelty statement is accurate and whether a
closer existing theorem invalidates or substantially narrows it.  This request
is about mathematical positioning, not verification of Lean syntax.

## Proposed novelty statement

> The contribution is an incidence-specific theorem package in which an
> equivalence generated by signed boundary observations is required to preserve
> an independently specified, possibly multi-valued ternary resonance relation.
> The package gives necessary and sufficient exact-descent conditions, a
> coequalizer/reflection universal property, positive and negative models, and a
> symmetry-reduced finite obstruction classification with `3 n^4` labelled
> obstructions and 32 support-minimal simplex automorphism orbits.

The paper does **not** claim novelty for quotient sets, relational congruences,
coalgebraic bisimulation, quotient coalgebras, coequalizers, free algebras,
many-sorted quotient algebras, incidence hypergraphs, automorphism reduction, or
finite executable enumeration individually.

## Five review questions

1. Is “incidence-generated equivalence plus an independent ternary relational
   congruence” already standard under another name?
2. Does a known coalgebraic quotient theorem subsume the exact three-coordinate
   descent iff without encoding the ternary relation into the coalgebra first?
3. Is the coequalizer/reflection result merely an instance of a standard
   relational-structure theorem in a way that should move it out of the main
   theorem?
4. Is there prior work classifying the same compatibility failures, or a more
   general obstruction theorem from which `3 n^4` and the 32 simplex orbits
   follow immediately?
5. Is the boundary-occurrence map to a three-sorted incidence hypergraph best
   described as a functorial forgetful encoding, or does established incidence
   terminology suggest a more precise formulation?

For every “yes” or “partly,” please provide the closest theorem number and
bibliographic reference and state which proposed claim must be removed or
narrowed.
